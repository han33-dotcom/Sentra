Day 28 · Personal baseline

Compared to themselves, not to a chart.

Each worker gets a personal normal built from their own last 28 days. The same reading can be routine for one person and a change for another, so Sentra looks for movement away from each person's pattern, not distance from a population average.

1 / 5 05:55 · Before the shift

Fit‑for‑shift readiness.

Sleep debt, resting heart rate and HRV are checked against the worker's own baseline before the first task. Green goes to work. Amber suggests a quick supervisor check-in. Red means a conversation before high-risk work. People make the call; Sentra gives them the signal.

2 / 5 10:40 · On shift

Exertion watch.

Heart rate that stays high for a sustained stretch of work, judged against the person's own range, raises a gentle flag on their wrist and on the supervisor's list. After a break, Sentra tracks recovery: how quickly heart rate settles back toward normal.

3 / 5 13:15 · Working alone

Lone-worker check.

If a band reports no movement for a long stretch during a shift, the worker gets a check-in prompt on their wrist. One tap says "I'm OK". No answer, and their supervisor is asked to check in person. Time limits are set per site and role.

4 / 5 Over 14 nights

Early-warning trends.

Breathing rate and skin temperature are followed night by night against the person's normal. A drift that lasts several nights becomes a flag for a check-in. It is a trend, never a diagnosis, and a single night never triggers anything.

5 / 5 17:30 · End of shift

Wear compliance.

Signals only count when the band is on. Sentra shows the hours each band was actually worn during the shift, so a gap shows up as a gap instead of being read as "all clear".

Trust & privacy

The worker's data stays the worker's.

The worker sees

All of their own data, their personal baseline, and every flag raised about them.

The employer sees

Flags, check-in status and aggregates. Not raw heart rate, sleep or temperature.

  • In-region hosting. Data is stored locally or in-region, built for GCC data-residency rules.
  • On-prem AI model. Flags are computed by a model that runs on your infrastructure, not an outside AI service.
  • Anonymized by default. Dashboards show pseudonymous IDs and aggregates.
  • Clinically informed. Thresholds and wording are built with input from occupational medicine physicians.

4 to 8 weeks · One site, one crew

What a pilot looks like.

  1. Week 0
    Kickoff and devices. We agree sites, roles and check-in rules with your HSE team, and supply bands for the pilot crew.
  2. Week 0
    Onboarding. Workers put the band on. No app, no account, no setup on their side.
  3. Weeks 1–4
    Baselines form. Personal normals build up and flags switch on as each person's history allows.
  4. Weekly
    Report. Readiness, flags, check-in times and wear compliance, every week.
  5. Week 4–8
    Review. Keep, extend or stop, based on what the reports show.
